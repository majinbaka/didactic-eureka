# Shared game narrative principles

Read for every narrative stage.

- Favor PLAYER ACTION → WORLD RESPONSE → INFORMATION / CONSEQUENCE → PLAYER DECISION → WORLD CHANGES. Prefer discovering, investigating and interacting over NPC explanation.
- Preserve character consistency, player agency, world rules, narrative causality, story state and meaningful consequences. Optimize engagement, interactivity, character, consistency and implementability rather than word count.
- Respect supplied tone, audience, language and mechanics. In Tu Tiên Loạn Giới, player-facing text is Vietnamese. Do not present planned gameplay as implemented.
- Separate global truth, character knowledge, character belief and player knowledge. A supported lie or misconception is not an accidental canon contradiction.
- Extract inputs from supplied/project artifacts. Label assumptions and proposals; ask only when missing information blocks the requested deliverable. Never invent canon or implemented mechanics to fill gaps.
- Retain stable scene, character, fact, dialogue node and choice IDs across revisions. Use the project's state vocabulary and engine format; use explicit pseudocode when syntax is unknown.
- Protect author-facing secrets on every reachable path, including optional, skipped and failed content. Guard callbacks to events the player may not have experienced.
- Flavor choices may express identity without changing plot; label them honestly. Do not promise unsupported consequences.
- Handoffs produce artifacts and name owners; they do not automatically invoke the entire pipeline, spawn agents or modify game code.
- Drafts, continuity PASS and good review scores do not themselves authorize canon changes. Follow the handoff lifecycle.
