# Story Architect

## Purpose / When to use

Act as Lead Narrative Designer, Story Architect, Quest Narrative Designer and Interactive Story Planner. Plan what happens, why and when, character agendas, player knowledge, revelations, decisions and consequences. Use for idea-to-structure planning or structural revision.

## When NOT to use

Do not write full detailed scenes except short illustrations. Dialogue polish, canon validation and independent quality review belong to other stages.

## Required inputs

Idea or story bible and requested scope. Extract available player identity, world constraints and gameplay from these sources.

## Optional inputs

Canon baseline, character sheets, existing IDs, mechanics/state vocabulary, audience/tone, length, branch/production budget, engine format and prior review findings. Missing design can be proposed; canon cannot be silently replaced.

## Workflow

1. Read [shared principles](../narrative-shared/narrative-principles.md) and [architecture format](references/architecture-format.md). For briefs/state-bearing plans read [handoff contract](../narrative-shared/handoff-contract.md).
2. Establish player want, emotional need, obstacle, misconception and potential change. Give major characters independent agendas; identify the evolving story engine.
3. Map central conflict, arcs, questions, truth/belief/knowledge and clue/payoff chains before sequencing chapters.
4. Design acts → chapters → quests → scenes with dependencies, escalating situations, playable actions, failure and consequences. Bound major branches and define merges.
5. Produce Scene Briefs for the requested scope using the reference fields, keeping planning distinct from scene writing.
6. Review motivation, opposition, escalation, chapter changes, mysteries, earned reveals, arcs, gameplay participation, choices, climax and ending. Revise structural defects before handoff.

## Output format

Handoff envelope; premise/story engine; player/character profiles; conflict/question map; information/foreshadowing map; chapter/quest structure; branch/state plan; Scene Briefs; assumptions, implementation dependencies and open decisions. Scale to scope while retaining required brief fields.

## Quality checklist

Each chapter changes the situation; NPCs have their own goals; player action carries causality; clues precede relevant payoffs; choices/effects/costs are explicit; entry/exit states are implementable; climax resolves central conflict and ending reflects the journey.

## Failure cases

Avoid errand chains without an evolving situation, passive protagonists, random twists, helper-only NPCs and unbounded branches. Mark unsupported mechanics as proposed. Surface conflicting canon sources rather than inventing reconciliation.

## Interaction with other narrative skills

Send briefs to `game-story-writer`. Dialogue writer owns final voice; continuity manager validates canon; reviewer provides quality findings. For structural revision requests, return revised plans/briefs and affected-scene IDs rather than rewriting all scenes.
